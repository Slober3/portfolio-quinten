## From Personal Range Logs to Computer Vision Research

As a licensed sport shooter affiliated with **VSK (Flemish Sport Shooting Federation)** at **LEDO Lier**, strict firearm safety and adherence to Belgian regulations always come first. To track my own progress across training sessions, I use existing tools such as **RangeBook** and **Shooting Companion** purely for personal use to record my own range sessions and consistency over time.

Reviewing paper ISSF 25m/50m targets after a session naturally sparked a technical R&D question: how reliably can standard Computer Vision turn a quick smartphone photo of a paper target into objective group-dispersion metrics (`Δr`)?

## Target-Scoring Pipeline Architecture (R&D Scope)

1. **Perspective Rectification:** A hand-held photo is rarely perpendicular to the target frame. Corner detection and homography warp the paper card back to normalized 2D coordinates.
2. **Concentric Ring Alignment & Shot Detection:** Concentric scoring rings are mapped against adaptive-thresholded hole contours, separating clean holes from overlapping edge hits.
3. **Trend & Biomechanics Insight:** Plotting center-of-group drift across sessions helps link breathing timing and trigger consistency directly to visible score stability.
